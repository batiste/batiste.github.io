Two Faces

Aeropolis, back to the core: one city on the edge of revolution, two characters per player, 2–4 players.

Playtest

You play both sides: a Citizen at the top of the Spire, an Outcast at its base. Each turn, take a card and tuck it above or below one of your characters: that column activates, every card in it. Send troops into the city and win its Uprisings: that is how your Outcast climbs. When your two characters meet, the game ends. You score the heights of both added together.

Setup

  1. Each player: a Citizen card and an Outcast card side by side; Citizen pawn on Spire , Outcast pawn on 1; ; troops in supply.
  2. Shuffle the Citizen and Outcast decks. Reveal cards from each: the row.
  3. Shuffle the Uprising deck and draw : the game lasts that many rounds. Reveal the first.

Round

  1. Starting with the first player, each player takes 1 turn, twice around the table.
  2. Resolve the Uprising, then Control and Catch-up (see City).
  3. The first player passes left. Reveal the next Uprising.

Turn

  • Recruit: take 1 card from the row. Pay its cost in goods; for each good you lack, Sink your Citizen 1. Tuck it above or below its character, then activate that side. Refill the row from the same deck.
  • If you cannot afford any card in the row, take 1 good of your choice instead.
  • Scheme (a strip effect and the Council Hall reward): look at the top card of each deck. Place 1 face down in front of you, the other at the bottom of its deck.

Empty deck: shuffle its discards into it.

Sides

Each character has 2 sides: tucked cards above it, tucked cards below it. A card tucked above shows only its top strip; below, only its bottom strip. A side holds strips: tucking another discards its oldest.

Activate: first take every "+" good on the side (strips and character). Then resolve the other effects: strips from newest to oldest, then the character's own strip. "→" effects are optional.

Icons

    Street Run

    Choose the Citizen or the Outcast deck, then draw from it one card at a time. Each gives the goods of its cost and adds its heat (printed on the card; free cards give nothing and run hot). Stop whenever you like and take the goods. If your total heat goes over your heat limit, you are caught: you gain nothing. Either way, discard the drawn cards. The decks differ: Citizen cards cost mostly Secrets and run hotter; Outcast cards cost mostly Stims and Papers.

    City

    Five locations. Troops sent by Deal go to the lower city, by Rally to the upper city; the Cable Lift is in both. Troops stay until they are lost or moved.

    Control: after each Uprising, the player with the most troops at a location takes its reward. Tie: nobody. At the Forum and the Cable Lift, the controller then loses 1 troop there (upkeep).

    Catch-up: then the player with the lowest score (all of them, if tied) sends 1 free troop to the lower city, from their supply. It keeps the last player in the fight for the next Uprisings.

    Uprising

    1. Each Uprising is fought at its location(s). Only players with troops there take part.
    2. Each fighter secretly commits any number of their Arms, then all fighters reveal them together with their Schemes.
    3. Strength = (your troops there + Arms committed), doubled for each ×2 Scheme, + your +N Schemes. Strongest gets the first reward, next gets the second. Tie: lower Outcast wins, then turn order.
    4. Every fighter, winner included, loses 1 troop at each of the Uprising's locations (back to supply). Fighters discard their Schemes and committed Arms; the others keep their Schemes.

    Spire

    • Your Outcast never goes above your Citizen. You cannot pay a cost that would put your Citizen below your Outcast.
    • When they stand on the same height, they meet.

    End

    • The game ends after the Uprising of the round in which any player's characters meet, or after round .
    • Score: the heights of your Citizen and Outcast added together. Tie: most Arms, then Stims, then Secrets, then Papers.

    Balance Notes

    Tuned with two-faces-sim.js: bots playing thousands of games on these exact rules (3 players). Bots show trends, not truths.

    • Costs in goods (was: Sink Citizen): paying in Citizen points meant every card lost score, so the cheapest player won and building did not pay. Now building pays: the best bots plan ahead, 80% of players use 3+ sides, and every card side wins 25–39% when taken (fair: 33%).
    • Score = Citizen + Outcast (was: the lower pawn).
    • Every fighter loses troops, the winner too: fewer blowouts.
    • Catch-up troop: fewer runaway leaders, more lead changes.
    • Council base +1 Paper, Patron ▼ 2 troops, Censor cheaper: weak sides; Orator costs more: too strong. Card sides: node two-faces-sim.js cards.
    • Scheme as a turn action removed (strips and Council Hall keep it): players and bots rarely chose it, and those who did won less. Early in the design it slowed the race; with goods costs it no longer matters.
    • Troop supply 12 (was 10): in a playtest, troops ran out. Leftover goods break final ties; converting them into points (5 goods = +1 Outcast) was tried and dropped: it widened gaps (2 players: blowouts 36% → 44%).
    • ×2 Schemes double troops + Arms only (was: the whole total, +N Schemes included): slightly tighter games; ×2 cards stay fair (29–35%). Not stacking ×2 was worse.
    • Tried and dropped: heat limit 4 or more Patrols, control without Spire steps, seat bonuses.
    • 5 rounds, Spire 14: shorter games are tighter (close finishes 44%, blowouts 21%), and characters meet in about 30% of games.
    • Street run through the Citizen or Outcast deck (was: a separate contraband deck): same balance, one deck less; free cards' heat makes goods scarcer. Base limit 6, hot free cards and +2/+3 limit strips: Street went from 1.6 to 7.8 runs per game.
    • Benefactor, Fixer (2 each): 2 goods → Rise Outcast 1. A sink for stockpiled goods. 3 → 1 was weaker; 4 → 2 made the card fair but widened gaps (leader wins more, fewer close games).
    • Tried and dropped: leader −2 Uprising strength (works, but needs checking who leads at the table).
    • Known issues: the leader after round 2 still wins about 45% (fair: 33%); street runs rarely bust (about 2%); Outcast troop strips (Fence ▼, Blackmailer ▼) are weak (22–26%).

    Playtest Questions

    • Game length: do characters meet before the last Uprising?
    • City: are troops too cheap or too expensive? Is the Council Hall (a free Scheme) worth fighting for?
    • Engine: what would make building 3 sides, or stacking one, worth it?
    • Cost: do specific goods costs make the street run and Council worth it?
    • Street run: how to make it risky? It rarely busts.
    • Scheme: too opaque? It slows the race, which keeps games tight.
    • Characters: all identical for now. Unique base strips per character later?
