Aeropolis - Two Faces

Aeropolis, back to the core: one city on the edge of revolution, two characters per player, 2–4 players.

Playtest

You play both sides: a Citizen at the top of the Spire, an Outcast at its base. Each turn, take a card and tuck it above or below one of your characters: that side activates, every card in it. Send troops into the city, win its Uprisings and hold its key locations: that is how your Outcast climbs. When your two characters meet, the game ends after that round. You score the heights of both added together.

Setup

  1. Each player takes a Citizen and an Outcast character (deal 2 of each, keep 1 of each) and places them side by side.
  2. Each player: Citizen pawn on Spire (the top), Outcast pawn on 1; ; troops in supply. Then apply your characters' setup (see Characters).
  3. Shuffle the Citizen and Outcast decks. Reveal cards from each: the row.
  4. Shuffle the Uprising deck and draw : the game lasts at most that many rounds. Reveal the first.
  5. Choose a first player.

Round

  1. Starting with the first player, each player takes 1 turn, twice around the table.
  2. Resolve the Uprising, then Control, then Catch-up (see Uprising and City).
  3. The first player passes left. Reveal the next Uprising.

Turn

  • Recruit: take 1 card from the row and pay its cost in goods; for each good you lack, Sink your Citizen 1 instead. Refill the row from the same deck. Tuck the card above or below its own character (Citizen cards go to the Citizen, Outcast cards to the Outcast), then activate that side.
  • If you cannot afford any card in the row, take 1 good of your choice instead.

Empty deck: shuffle its discards into it.

Sides

Each character has 2 sides: cards tucked above it and cards tucked below it. A card tucked above shows only its top strip; below, only its bottom strip. A side holds strips: tucking another discards its oldest.

Activate: first take every good the side produces (goods not behind an arrow, on strips and character). Then resolve the other effects: strips from newest to oldest, then the character's own strip. "→" effects are optional: pay what is left of the arrow to get what is right of it.

Characters

A character's own strips are the base of its sides: they resolve last on every activation (after the goods). The Councillor and the Hustler are the plain ones; the others trade something for a specialty.

Icons

Goods are public. Every good also pays card costs.

    Street Run

    The Outcast's own strip above it (Street) runs the street. Your heat limit is the number on that strip, plus every 🔥 strip resolved before it in the same activation. Choose the Citizen or the Outcast deck, then draw from it one card at a time. Each card gives the goods of its cost and adds its printed heat; free cards give nothing and run hot.

    • Stop whenever you like and take the goods.
    • If your total heat goes over your limit, you are caught: you gain nothing.
    • After drawing a card, you may spend a Stim to discard it; you must then draw again.
    • Either way, discard the drawn cards. Citizen cards give mostly Secrets and Papers; Outcast cards mostly Stims, Papers and Arms.

    City

    Five locations, in the lower city, the upper city, or both (the Cable Lift). A troop icon says where the troop goes: any location of that city. Troops stay until they are lost or moved; "Move" goes to any location, and you may move fewer. With an empty supply, a troop effect does nothing.

    Control: after each Uprising, the player with the most troops at a location takes its reward. Tie: nobody. At the Forum and the Cable Lift, the controller then loses 1 troop there (upkeep).

    Catch-up: then the player with the lowest score (all of them, if tied) sends 1 free troop to the lower city, from their supply. It keeps the last player in the fight.

    Uprising

    Scheme (a strip effect, and the Council Hall reward): look at the top card of each deck. Place 1 face down in front of you, the other at the bottom of its deck. Its Scheme value (+2, +3 or ×2) stays hidden until an Uprising.

    1. Each Uprising is fought at its location(s). Only players with troops there take part.
    2. Each fighter secretly commits any number of their Arms. Then all fighters reveal them, together with all their Schemes (not optional).
    3. Strength = (your troops there + Arms committed), doubled for each ×2 Scheme, + your +N Schemes. Strongest gets the first reward, next gets the second. Tie: most leftover goods, as at the end of the game (Arms after committing, then Stims, Secrets, Papers), then turn order.
    4. Every fighter, winner included, loses 1 troop at each of the Uprising's locations where they have one (back to supply). Fighters discard their Schemes and committed Arms; the others keep their Schemes.

    Spire

    • Heights go from 1 to . Your Citizen never goes above , and your Outcast never goes above your Citizen: any extra rise is lost.
    • You cannot pay a cost that would put your Citizen below your Outcast.
    • When your Citizen and Outcast stand on the same height, they meet.

    End

    • When any player's characters meet, the round is still played to its end: the remaining turns, the Uprising, Control and Catch-up. Then the game ends. Otherwise it ends after round .
    • Score: the heights of your Citizen and Outcast added together. Tie: most Arms, then Stims, then Secrets, then Papers.

    Balance Notes

    Tuned with two-faces-sim.js: bots playing thousands of games on these exact rules (3 players). Bots show trends, not truths.

    • Costs in goods (was: Sink Citizen): paying in Citizen points meant every card lost score, so the cheapest player won and building did not pay. Now building pays: the best bots plan ahead, 80% of players use 3+ sides, and every card side wins 25–39% when taken (fair: 33%).
    • Score = Citizen + Outcast (was: the lower pawn).
    • Every fighter loses troops, the winner too: fewer blowouts.
    • Catch-up troop: fewer runaway leaders, more lead changes.
    • Council base +1 Paper, Patron ▼ 2 troops, Censor cheaper: weak sides; Orator costs more: too strong. Card sides: node two-faces-sim.js cards.
    • Scheme as a turn action removed (strips and Council Hall keep it): players and bots rarely chose it, and those who did won less. Early in the design it slowed the race; with goods costs it no longer matters.
    • Troop supply 12 (was 10): in a playtest, troops ran out. Leftover goods break final ties; converting them into points (5 goods = +1 Outcast) was tried and dropped: it widened gaps (2 players: blowouts 36% → 44%).
    • ×2 Schemes double troops + Arms only (was: the whole total, +N Schemes included): slightly tighter games; ×2 cards stay fair (29–35%). Not stacking ×2 was worse.
    • Characters tuned with node two-faces-sim.js characters (random distinct characters, smart bots): all within about 2 points of fair at 2, 3 and 4 players; pairs within 28–39% at 3 players. First drafts: Gunsmith (+2 Arms) and Demagogue (Forum troop) won 43–45%, Chancellor 25%.
    • Tried and dropped: heat limit 4 or more Patrols, control without Spire steps, seat bonuses.
    • 5 rounds, Spire 14: shorter games are tighter; most games now end by a meeting in round 4 or 5.
    • Street run through the Citizen or Outcast deck (was: a separate contraband deck): same balance, one deck less; free cards' heat makes goods scarcer. Base limit 6, hot free cards and +2/+3 limit strips made the Street worth taking.
    • Benefactor, Fixer (2 each): 2 goods → Rise Outcast 1. A sink for stockpiled goods. 3 → 1 was weaker; 4 → 2 made the card fair but widened gaps (leader wins more, fewer close games).
    • Tried and dropped: leader −2 Uprising strength (works, but needs checking who leads at the table).
    • Forum and Cable Lift upkeep (−1 troop for the controller): holding them uncontested wears down; 2 players: fewer blowouts, more comebacks.
    • Known issues: the leader after round 2 still wins about 45% (fair: 33%; 2 players: about 55% vs 50%); street runs rarely bust; Outcast troop strips (Fence ▼, Blackmailer ▼) are weak; a Citizen rise at the top is lost.

    Playtest Questions

    • Ending: is triggering the meeting a good gamble, given that the others still play the round?
    • City: is the Council Hall (a Scheme each round) too strong when nobody contests it? Should Schemes be placed on a location?
    • Engine: what would make building 3 sides, or stacking one, worth it?
    • Street run: how to make it riskier? It rarely busts.
    • Rise Citizen at the top is lost: should it become an Outcast rise?
    • Characters: are they balanced, and do they play differently? Which pairs are too strong?
